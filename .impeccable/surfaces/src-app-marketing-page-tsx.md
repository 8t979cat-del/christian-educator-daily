# Surface: home page (/)

Mode: Persuade. Audience: a school administrator deciding on a founding pilot, and a teacher who opens the page each morning to present today's devotional without signing in. Action: present today's devotional (working, on the page); secondary: become a founding school. Proof: the real day's devotional, presentable; the same Scripture at different reading levels (labelled sample). Constraints: no invented customers, prices or content; WCAG 2.2 AA; works on Chromebooks and phones. The "180 days" count stays in the background: the devotionals follow the shape of a school year, but the promise is one for every school day, whatever the school's calendar.

## Direction contract

THESIS: Each day is a lesson in a classroom workbook: an edtech course page meets the Bible on paper. It refuses both the SaaS landing page (centred hero, card grid, dark CTA band) and the hymnal-and-letterpress costume it replaces.

OWN-WORLD: Bright workbook paper with faint blue ruling and a red margin line; ink-navy text. A confident classroom blue for actions, a gold for progress and "done", Bible red only for verse numbers. Lexend (built for reading fluency) for interface and headings; Literata, a screen-reading book serif, for Scripture and devotional text. Friendly rounded controls and lesson cards with soft offset shadows, step tabs, progress dots, rounded icons.

STORY: A teacher sees today's devotional as a four-step lesson (Read, Talk, Do, Pray) and presents it in one click. An administrator sees it already set at four reading levels and laid out week by week through the school year, then writes to become a founding school.

FIRST VIEWPORT: A white app bar with the wordmark and navigation. Below it, today's lesson card on ruled paper: the date and "This week: theme · Day n of 5" with five progress dots; the title in Lexend; four step tabs (Read, Talk, Do, Pray); the working inline presenter showing Scripture in Literata with red verse numbers; a blue "Present to class" primary button. Beside it on desktop, a "Why schools use it" panel with one line of pitch and the founding link; it stacks below on phones.
